Bali is easy to love and surprisingly easy to get wrong. Traffic is slow, every area feels completely different, and the "must-see" spots are crowded by 9 AM. This guide covers everything our trip captains tell travellers before they fly — so your first trip feels like your fifth.

Best time to visit

The dry season from April to October brings sunny days, low humidity and calm seas for island hopping. July and August are peak months, so book stays early. November to March is the wet season — short afternoon showers, greener landscapes and lower prices.

Trip captain tip: May, June and September give you dry-season weather without the peak-season crowds or prices.

Where to stay

Most first-timers split their week between the cultural heart and the coast.

UbudRice terraces, temples, yoga & cafés. 3 nights.
SeminyakBeach clubs, boutiques & sunsets. 2 nights.
UluwatuCliff views, surf & hidden coves. 2 nights.
"Do Ubud first, beaches last. Ending your trip by the ocean is the best cure for post-holiday blues."

Getting around

  • Private driver: ₹3,000–3,500 a day — the most comfortable way to see the island.
  • Ride apps: Grab and Gojek work in most areas, though some zones restrict pick-ups.
  • Scooter: Cheap and fun, but only if you're an experienced rider with an international licence.

Visa & entry

Indian passport holders can get a visa on arrival or apply for an e-VOA online before flying. A small tourist levy is also payable on arrival. Rules change often — always check the official immigration website before you travel.

What to pack

  • Light cotton clothes and a sarong (needed for temple visits)
  • Reef-safe sunscreen and insect repellent
  • Sturdy shoes for the Mount Batur trek
  • Universal adapter — Bali uses Type C/F sockets
